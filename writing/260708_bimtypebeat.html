Working Title for an Article on the Nature of BIM and Design Software Users

BIM users are not design-focused. Crazy hot take I know. But as I sit here and think about the nature of the Architecture field I realized something (or rather remembered); Revit is not a design software. No matter what people or even Autodesk try to make it be, it will never be a design software. Whether that be FormIt or generative design in Revit, the attempts that Autodesk has made will never make it a design software. Furthermore, I would go on to say that those who are solely designing in Revit are not design-focused, but more importantly not designing to their fullest potential. Trying to fit a square peg in a round hole yadayada, but I wholeheartedly believe it to be true. The Revit vs. Rhino debate is comparing two completely unlike things with each other. Many people have made this argument already, but I feel like it's more important than ever in this moment. While I've been watching tens Revit videos, the demographic difference is drastic; it's a lot of old heads, no shade. It's a generational difference partially, which poses the question of what the next software or tool is going to create the next gap between age groups. It's crazy to think that the people using Rhino and Revit are even in the same field, but they are. The users of both softwares very much so depend on eachother and their specific strengths. Design is art and those trying to make art with Revit are misusing it. Relatedely, sketching will never be replaced by Revit, nor any other modeler like Sketchup or Rhino. Trace, sketching, and other means of generative design will never be replaced by Revit, and the majority of those who skip out on this stage entirely are a hinderance to the built environment. Why would anyone use a documentation tool to create art. For some designers the tool does not matter, like those who use Microsoft Paint to create beautiful art. But they are not the majority.
